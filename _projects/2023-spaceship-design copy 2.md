For this project, our group performed a fluid-mechanical dissection of a Keurig single-serve coffee machine to understand how its internal fluid systems are designed and operated. We physically dismantled the machine using basic tools including a screwdriver, hammer, and scissors to expose the internal components, focusing on the pump, tubing, heating pathway, and flow restrictions that govern water delivery through the pod. We examined how fluid-mechanical principles such as pressure generation, pipe flow, losses, and Reynolds number considerations influence the machine’s performance and design choices. Throughout the dissection, we documented the geometry, flow paths, and components, and produced a short explanatory video presenting our findings and analysis using the language and framework of fluid mechanics.
